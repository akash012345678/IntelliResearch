Plant Disease Detection Using an Innovative
Swin-Axial Transformer

ABSTRACT Plant diseases are a significant threat to global agricultural production, and accurate and
efficient disease detection is crucial for ensuring food security. With the rapid development of deep
learning technology, Transformer architectures have demonstrated strong global feature extraction
capabilities in image classification and detection tasks. However, traditional Transformer models still face
high computational costs and large parameter volumes when handling multi-scale diseases and complex
backgrounds. To address this challenge, this paper proposes the Swin-Axial Transformer model based on
the Swin Transformer. By introducing the TokenEmbedder module, the number of tokens is reduced, and
multi-scale deep convolution is used to efficiently extract image features, significantly lowering
computational costs. Furthermore, the Axial Transformer module reduces computational complexity by
restricting self-attention calculations to local regions along the axial direction. With the combination of the
Axial Compression module and the Detail Enhancement module, the model can efficiently extract global
semantic features and effectively supplement local detail information. Compared to traditional global self-
attention mechanisms, the Axial Transformer successfully avoids computational bottlenecks through axial
compression strategies. Experimental results show that the model achieves a precision of 79.52% on the
PlantDoc dataset, 99.79% on the PlantVillage dataset, and 95.69% on a self-constructed fusion dataset. The
model's parameter size is 14.87 M with a computational load of 2.96 GMac, which represents a reduction of
46% and 32%, respectively, compared to the Swin Transformer-T model, significantly improving
computational efficiency. The model presented in this paper provides an efficient and reliable solution for
large-scale crop disease detection.
INDEX TERMS Plant disease detection, deep learning, global feature extraction, multi-scale features.
I. INTRODUCTION
Plant diseases pose a significant challenge to modern
agriculture, with globally estimated grain yield reduction
caused by diseases exceeding 10%. Timely and effective
disease detection is crucial for preventing the spread of
diseases and mitigating their impacts. However, plant
disease detection presents unique challenges compared to
other object or pattern detection tasks. Disease symptoms
often vary significantly across different plant species, and
their
manifestations
are
strongly
influenced
by
environmental
factors
such
as
changes
in
light,
temperature, and humidity. This makes plant disease
detection more complex and challenging. Currently, most
disease detection still relies on manual field evaluations
by crop pathology experts, a process that is not only
subjective, time-consuming, and labor-intensive but also
prone
to
omissions
and
misjudgments.
By
contrast,
machine learning, particularly deep learning methods, has
emerged as a vital tool for plant disease diagnosis. In
recent years, image classification techniques have been
widely applied in crop disease detection. Traditional
image classification methods often depend on manually
designed
features,
whose
selection
and
extraction
accuracy
directly
affect
classification
performance.
Factors such as lighting variations and background noise
in natural environments further degrade image quality,
exacerbating the impact on classification results.
In recent years, machine learning methods, particularly
those used for image classification, have emerged as vital
tools for crop disease diagnosis. Common techniques
include
image
segmentation,
feature
extraction,
and
pattern classification to categorize disease images. For

F. Ablation Experiments
To evaluate the impact of different modules in the Swin-
Axial Transformer model on the overall classification
performance, this study designed eight sets of comparative
experiments. By systematically removing or replacing key
modules, the contribution of each module to the model's
performance was carefully analyzed. The experimental
results detailed the impact of these modifications on model
parameters, computational complexity, and F1 score (see
Table 3).
The first experiment used Swin-T as the baseline model,
with an F1 score of 75.02%, a parameter count of 27.54 M,
and a computational complexity of 4.38GMac. Although the
baseline model demonstrated strong global feature extraction
ability, its parameter count and computational complexity
were relatively high.
The second experiment further verified the importance of
the TokenEmbedder module by removing it. The results
showed that the F1 score dropped to 71.68%, highlighting the
crucial role of the TokenEmbedder module in enhancing
model performance. Although the model's parameter count
decreased to 15.14 M and computational complexity dropped
to 3.16 GMac, the absence of this module significantly
affected the classification performance.
Experiments 3 to 6 deeply verified the unique advantages
of the module in this paper in plant disease feature modeling
by replacing the TokenEmbedder module with mainstream
attention mechanisms such as CBAM [33], EMA [34],
ECA[35], and CoTAttention [36]. The comparison results
showed that existing attention mechanisms have significant
limitations in balancing multi-scale feature fusion efficiency
and
computational
complexity.
The
CBAM
module
(Experiment 3), as a combination of channel and spatial
attention, although it enhanced feature selection ability
through a dual attention mechanism, its F1 score (74.32%)
was still 3.76% lower than the model in this paper. This is
because CBAM relies on independent attention calculations
for each channel and each spatial location. When dealing
with multi-scale disease spots commonly found in plant
disease images (such as the coexistence of millimeter-level
spots and centimeter-level spots), it is difficult to achieve
efficient cross-scale feature interaction, and its computational
complexity (3.16GMac) is not significantly lower than that of
the TokenEmbedder module. The performance decline of the
EMA and ECA modules (Experiments 4 and 5) was more
obvious, with F1 scores of 72.12% and 71.87% respectively,
exposing the limitations of single-dimensional attention
mechanisms: EMA only focuses on weight adjustment
between channels, ignoring the key role of spatial features
such as disease spot morphology and texture; although ECA
optimizes computational efficiency through dimensionality
reduction, it is limited by a fixed receptive field and cannot
capture the structural associations of disease regions at
different
scales,
resulting
in
insufficient
feature
discrimination
under
complex
backgrounds.
The
CoTAttention module (Experiment 6) learns a dynamic
attention matrix by mining contextual information around the
key, effectively strengthening the model's representation
ability, achieving an F1 score of 72.64%, which outperforms
the EMA and ECA modules. However, this module has
obvious
disadvantages
in
computational
resource
consumption. Both the parameter count (16.40 M) and
computational complexity (3.42 GMac) are higher than those
of the model in this paper, failing to achieve a good balance
between feature expression and computational efficiency.
In contrast, the TokenEmbedder module, through a multi-
scale depth convolution kernel group and a fixed-number
token
generation
mechanism,
while
improving
feature
extraction
efficiency,
maintains
low
computational
complexity. This module can efficiently fuse multi-scale
features when processing plant disease images and avoids the
deficiencies of a single attention mechanism in cross-scale
feature interaction and computational resource consumption,
thus achieving a good balance between F1 score and
computational efficiency.
The seventh experiment replaced the Axial Transformer
module with the standard Transformer module, providing a
direct performance comparison between the Axial-Squeeze
Attention mechanism and the traditional multi-head attention
mechanism. The experimental results showed that although
the standard Transformer module, with a global self-attention
mechanism, achieved an F1 score of 76.53%, which is lower
than the proposed model, it had a significant disadvantage in
computational efficiency. Its parameter count was 15.58 M,
and the computational complexity was 3.01 GMac, both of
which were higher than the proposed model's 14.87 M and
2.96 GMac, respectively.
Unlike the standard Transformer's global self-attention
mechanism (with a computational complexity of O(N²), the
Axial Transformer module proposed in this paper uses the
Axial-Squeeze Attention mechanism, which limits self-
attention calculations to local regions along the horizontal
and vertical axes. By compressing features along each axis
and fusing information across axes, the computational
complexity is optimized to O(N). Moreover, the Axial-
Squeeze Attention combines the Axial Compression module
and the Detail Enhancement module, which efficiently
extracts global semantic features and supplements local detail

With Token Dimensions increasing from 128 to 768, the

Score

maintaining
relatively
low
computational

The Swin-Axial Transformer model proposed in this paper
effectively addresses the high computational costs, complex
backgrounds, and multi-scale feature extraction challenges in
plant disease detection tasks. Unlike traditional object or
pattern detection tasks, plant disease detection not only
requires identifying and classifying disease types but also

must deal with complex natural environmental backgrounds,

and
these

often
intertwine with complex backgrounds, further complicating
disease detection.To effectively tackle this challenge, this
paper introduces the TokenEmbedder module, which reduces
the number of tokens and combines multi-scale deep
convolution to efficiently extract image features. This
improvement significantly lowers computational complexity
while optimizing the feature extraction process. The module
provides a more compact and efficient input for subsequent
models, further enhancing overall computational efficiency.
Furthermore, the Axial Transformer module introduced in
this paper reduces computational complexity by restricting
self-attention calculations to local regions along the axial
directions. At the same time, by combining the Axial
Compression module and the Detail Enhancement module,
the Axial Transformer efficiently extracts global semantic
features and supplements local detail information. The
synergistic
effect
of
these
two
modules
significantly
enhances the feature modeling capabilities of the Axial
Transformer
in
complex
backgrounds
and
multi-scale
scenarios.
Experimental
results
show
that
the
Swin-Axial
Transformer outperforms multiple datasets with significant
advantages. For instance, on the PlantDoc dataset, the model

Additionally, the model has 14.87 M parameters and a
computational load of 2.96 GMac, which represents a 46%
reduction
in
parameters
and
a
32%
reduction
in
computational load compared to the Swin Transformer-T
model, significantly improving computational efficiency.
These results indicate that the Swin-Axial Transformer not
only surpasses several mainstream models in classification
accuracy but also demonstrates significant advantages in
terms of computational resource consumption. The model
presented in this paper provides an efficient and reliable
solution for large-scale crop disease detection, with broad
application prospects.
However, this study also acknowledges certain limitations.
First,
although
the
Swin-Axial
Transformer
has
been
optimized for computational efficiency, it still requires
substantial computational resources when processing high-
resolution images and large-scale datasets. Second, the
current model architecture is primarily optimized for specific
datasets, and there may be a risk of performance degradation
when
extended
to
more
diverse
diseases
and
crops.
Furthermore, although the model performs excellently on
several experimental datasets, its robustness may be affected
in more complex backgrounds, lighting changes, and other
scenarios, leading to unstable performance in real-world
applications. Finally, although the Swin-Axial Transformer
has made progress in real-time processing, its real-time
response capability may still be constrained by computational
resources in resource-limited environments, especially in
real-time disease monitoring systems, where it may not fully
meet the demands for low latency and efficient inference.
Future research can improve the Swin-Axial Transformer
model's generalization and scalability in several directions.
First, to further optimize computational efficiency and real-
time performance, lightweight model techniques such as
pruning,
quantization,
and
model
distillation
can
be
employed to reduce computational resource consumption,
while combining hardware acceleration (such as GPUs or
TPUs) to improve inference speed. These optimizations will
make the model suitable not only for plant disease detection
tasks but also extend its applicability to real-time inference
fields, such as video surveillance, autonomous driving, and
more.
Additionally,
adopting
distributed
computing
architectures and edge computing strategies can distribute
computational tasks across multiple nodes, reducing the
burden on a single device and ensuring low-latency and
efficient inference in resource-constrained environments,
further enhancing the model's versatility.Second, to enhance
the
model's
scalability
and
generalizability,
multi-task
learning and cross-domain transfer learning strategies can be
implemented, allowing
the Swin-Axial Transformer to
maintain high performance and stability when handling a
broader range of object and pattern detection tasks. For
example, pretraining the model on different object detection
datasets and fine-tuning it for new tasks will help the model
handle complex backgrounds and multi-scale issues in plant
disease detection, as well as perform excellently in fields like
medical imaging, industrial defect detection, and others.
Through this approach, the model can more flexibly adapt to
variations across different domains.Finally, to improve the
model's robustness, future research can focus on optimizing
data augmentation techniques and self-supervised learning
methods,
ensuring
that
the
model
maintains
efficient
detection capabilities even in complex backgrounds, lighting
changes, and partial occlusions. These improvements will
enhance the Swin-Axial Transformer's adaptability and
generalization
across
various
complex
environments,
meeting the needs of a wider range of object and pattern
detection tasks, and providing more stable and precise
solutions for large-scale, real-time monitoring systems.
